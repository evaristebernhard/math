# Residual-36: complete exclusion of the finite normal quadratic target

Date: 2026-08-09

Status: **superseded as the complete branch proof by `TRIANGLE_R36_QUADRATIC_TARGET_JORDAN_CLOSURE_2026-08-09.md`.** The rank-four classification and smooth-line counts below remain valid, but this note assumes the earlier rank-three reduction; the newer note replaces that reduction by a separate self-adjoint Jordan classification and avoids any special-fibre count on the singular conic.

## 0. Setup

Continue the disjoint-net residual-36 global map
\[
\phi:Y=\operatorname{Bl}_A\mathbf P^3\longrightarrow X'\subset\mathbf P^5,
\qquad |A|=28,
\]
with degree
\[
\deg\phi=9,
\]
and assume the quadratic-target case
\[
X'=Q_0\cap Q_1,
\qquad (m,e)=(2,9),
\]
where `Q_0` is smooth.  Assume throughout this note that `X'` is normal and `phi` is finite.

The previous reduction already proves that the pencil contains a rank-four member
\[
Q_\lambda
\]
whose radical line
\[
L=\mathbf P(K),\qquad K=\ker Q_\lambda,
\]
is contained in `X'`.  We now show that this model is itself impossible.

The argument has two ingredients:

1. classify the possible `T`-invariant Lagrangian planes in the rank-four pencil, where
   \[
   T=B_0^{-1}B_1
   \]
   is self-adjoint for the nondegenerate form `B_0`;
2. count exceptional planes along smooth intersection lines.  If two target planes meet along a line whose generic point is smooth on `X'`, the degree-nine finite map can supply at most nine exceptional-plane sheets along that line.

The resulting inequalities contradict the existence of all 28 exceptional divisors.

---

## 1. The smooth-line sheet bound

For each `a in A`, transversality of the two determinant complete intersections gives
\[
E_a\simeq\mathbf P^2\xrightarrow{\sim}\Pi_a\subset X'.
\]
Let `Pi_i,Pi_j` be two common planes in `X'` and suppose
\[
\ell=\Pi_i\cap\Pi_j
\]
is a line whose generic point is smooth on `X'`.

Let `n_i` (resp. `n_j`) be the number of exceptional divisors mapping to `Pi_i` (resp. `Pi_j`).  Every such exceptional plane contains the inverse image of `ell` as a line, and distinct exceptional divisors are disjoint.  Hence at the generic point `eta_ell` these exceptional divisors contribute `n_i+n_j` distinct degree-one sheets.

The local ring `O_{X',eta_ell}` is a regular local ring of dimension two.  The local rings of `Y` above `eta_ell` are Cohen--Macaulay of dimension two.  Since `phi` is finite, miracle flatness makes the localization finite free; its total rank is the global degree nine.  Therefore
\[
\boxed{n_i+n_j\le9.}\tag{SL}
\]

This is the only covering-theoretic input used below.

---

## 2. Self-adjoint primary decomposition

Let `V` be the six-dimensional vector space underlying `P^5`.  Since
\[
Q_1(x,y)=B_0(Tx,y),
\]
a common plane `P(L) subset X'` is exactly a three-dimensional `B_0`-Lagrangian subspace `L subset V` satisfying
\[
TL\subset L.
\]

The rank-four pencil member corresponds to an eigenvalue `lambda` for which
\[
K=\ker(T-\lambda),\qquad \dim K=2.
\]
Because its radical line lies on the smooth quadric `Q_0`,
\[
B_0|_K=0.
\]
Thus the `lambda`-primary part has two Jordan blocks, each of size at least two.

Generalized eigenspaces for distinct eigenvalues are mutually `B_0`-orthogonal and nondegenerate.  Any invariant Lagrangian splits among the primary summands.  Since its dimension is exactly one half of `dim V`, every primary summand occurring in the decomposition must have even dimension and must contribute a half-dimensional invariant isotropic subspace.

Consequently the rank-four singular-line case has only the following Jordan possibilities:

\[
\boxed{
(J_2,J_2)_\lambda+(J_2)_\mu,
\quad
(J_2,J_2)_\lambda+(J_1,J_1)_\mu,
\quad
(J_2,J_4)_\lambda,
\quad
(J_3,J_3)_\lambda.
}
\tag{J}
\]

Here `(J_1,J_1)_mu` means that the two-dimensional `mu`-primary space is semisimple, i.e. `T=mu I` there.

For a nilpotent Jordan block `J_m`, choose a chain
\[
e_1,\ldots,e_m,
\qquad Ne_i=e_{i-1},
\]
with `e_1` in the kernel and use the standard self-adjoint anti-diagonal form
\[
B(e_i,e_j)=\delta_{i+j,m+1}.
\]
The invariant Lagrangians can then be written explicitly.

## 3. Type `(J2,J2)_lambda + (J2)_mu`

On the `lambda`-primary four-space choose chains
\[
e_1,e_2,\qquad f_1,f_2.
\]
The invariant Lagrangian two-spaces are exactly
\[
L_0=\langle e_1,f_1\rangle,
\]
and
\[
L_\pm
=\langle e_1\pm i f_1,\ e_2\pm i f_2\rangle.
\]
Indeed an invariant two-space different from `L_0` is generated by `v,Nv`; isotropy is the single equation `a^2+b^2=0`, giving the two signs.

The `mu`-Jordan block has a unique invariant isotropic line `M`.  Hence `X'` contains exactly three common planes
\[
\Pi_0=\mathbf P(L_0\oplus M),
\qquad
\Pi_\pm=\mathbf P(L_\pm\oplus M).
\]

For each sign,
\[
\Pi_0\cap\Pi_\pm
\]
is a line.  Its generic point is smooth: the singular locus of an intersection of two quadrics consists of isotropic eigenvectors of `T`; the displayed line contains generalized `lambda`-vectors and is not contained in an eigenspace.

Writing the exceptional-plane multiplicities as `n_0,n_+,n_-`, (SL) gives
\[
n_0+n_+\le9,
\qquad
n_0+n_-\le9.
\]
Together with `n_+,n_-\le9`, this gives in particular
\[
n_0+n_++n_-\le18<28,
\]
a contradiction.

## 4. Type `(J2,J2)_lambda + (J1,J1)_mu`

The `lambda`-primary invariant Lagrangians are again
\[
L_0,L_+,L_-.
\]
On the semisimple two-dimensional `mu`-primary quadratic space there are exactly two isotropic lines
\[
M_+,M_-.
\]
Thus the common planes are
\[
\Pi_{i,\varepsilon}=\mathbf P(L_i\oplus M_\varepsilon),
\qquad
 i\in\{0,+,-\},\quad \varepsilon\in\{+,-\}.
\]
Let their exceptional multiplicities be `n_{i,epsilon}`.

The following six pairs meet along lines whose generic points are smooth:
\[
(\Pi_{+, +},\Pi_{+,-}),
\qquad
(\Pi_{-, +},\Pi_{-,-}),
\]
and, for each `epsilon`,
\[
(\Pi_{0,\varepsilon},\Pi_{+,\varepsilon}),
\qquad
(\Pi_{0,\varepsilon},\Pi_{-,\varepsilon}).
\]
The only same-`i` intersection that is the singular radical line is
\[
\Pi_{0,+}\cap\Pi_{0,-}=\mathbf P(K),
\]
and it is deliberately not used.

Applying (SL) to the six smooth lines and summing all six inequalities, every multiplicity occurs exactly twice.  Hence
\[
2\sum_{i,\varepsilon}n_{i,\varepsilon}\le6\cdot9=54,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{\sum n_{i,\varepsilon}\le27},
\]
contradicting the 28 exceptional divisors.

This is the numerically sharp case: the degree-nine bound misses 28 by exactly one.

## 5. Type `(J2,J4)_lambda`

Choose a length-two chain `e_1,e_2` and a length-four chain
\[
f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4.
\]
There are exactly three invariant Lagrangian three-spaces:
\[
L_0=\langle e_1,f_1,f_2\rangle,
\]
and
\[
L_\pm
=\langle
f_3\pm i e_2,
\ f_2\pm i e_1,
\ f_1
\rangle.
\]

Indeed an invariant Lagrangian with two-dimensional kernel must contain `K=span(e_1,f_1)` and the isotropy conditions force the third generator to be `f_2`; a cyclic length-three invariant Lagrangian is generated by `f_3+a e_2`, and isotropy gives `1+a^2=0`.

The intersections
\[
\mathbf P(L_0)\cap\mathbf P(L_\pm)
\]
are smooth lines generically.  Thus if the corresponding exceptional multiplicities are `n_0,n_+,n_-`,
\[
n_0+n_+\le9,
\qquad
n_0+n_-\le9,
\]
and hence
\[
\sum n_i\le18<28.
\]
Contradiction.

## 6. Type `(J3,J3)_lambda`

Choose length-three chains
\[
e_1,e_2,e_3,
\qquad
f_1,f_2,f_3.
\]
There are exactly four invariant Lagrangian three-spaces.

Two have nilpotent type `(2,1)`:
\[
A_\pm
=\langle e_1,f_1,e_2\pm i f_2\rangle.
\]
Two are cyclic of length three:
\[
B_\pm
=\langle
 e_3\pm i f_3,
 e_2\pm i f_2,
 e_1\pm i f_1
\rangle.
\]
The equations `1+a^2=0` again give the two signs in each case.

The intersections
\[
\mathbf P(A_+)\cap\mathbf P(B_+),
\qquad
\mathbf P(A_-)\cap\mathbf P(B_-)
\]
are lines, and their generic points are smooth because these lines are not contained in the eigenspace `K`.  Therefore, with multiplicities `a_+,a_-,b_+,b_-`,
\[
a_++b_+\le9,
\qquad
a_-+b_-\le9.
\]
Consequently
\[
a_++a_-+b_++b_-\le18<28,
\]
again impossible.

## 7. Theorem

Every possible self-adjoint Jordan type compatible with a normal rank-four singular-line quadratic target contradicts the existence of the 28 exceptional planes under a finite degree-nine map.  Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
(m,e)=(2,9),\quad X'\text{ normal},\quad \phi\text{ finite}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \bot.
}
\]

Equivalently, the finite normal quadratic-target branch of residual distance 36 is completely excluded.

Combining this with the existing cubic-target Euler theorem gives the current normal residual-36 state:

\[
\boxed{
\text{normal residual-36 survivor}
\Longrightarrow
\text{nonfinite map}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\bigl((m,e)=(3,6)\text{ finite with singular curve}\bigr).
}
\]

Nonnormal targets remain separate.

## 8. Audit notes

- The proof does **not** use the earlier clean `(1,3)/(2,2)` carrier classification.
- It does **not** use a bound on special fibres over singular points.
- All degree-nine counting is performed over generic points of lines contained in the smooth locus of the normal target, where finite flatness is justified by regularity of the target local ring and Cohen--Macaulayness of the smooth source.
- The invariant-Lagrangian description is the same self-adjoint pencil formalism used in the standard theory of intersections of quadrics; the explicit dimension-six list above is included so that the argument does not depend on an external classification theorem.
