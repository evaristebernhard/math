# Cover letter draft — Journal of Algebraic Geometry

Dear Editors of the Journal of Algebraic Geometry,

We would like to submit our manuscript

**“Low-weight even sets of nodes on octic surfaces: divisible codes, symmetric determinants, and comparison geometry”**

for consideration for publication in the *Journal of Algebraic Geometry*.

The paper studies low-weight strict even sets of nodes on complex octic surfaces, motivated by the extremal problem of whether the maximal number of nodes on an octic surface is \(168\). Starting from the strict even-node code of a hypothetical octic with at least \(169\) nodes, the coding-theoretic reduction leaves minimum weights \(48\), \(64\), and \(72\).

The manuscript proves, among other results, the following. The minimum-distance-\(72\) branch is excluded by an exact MacWilliams--Krawtchouk certificate. Every strict weight-\(64\) even set is shown to be a reduced transverse \(CI(4,4,4)\) and to admit a symmetric \(2\times2\) determinantal presentation of the octic. In the minimum-\(64\) case, exact coding bounds give \(A_{64}\ge 533\) and allow a global reduction to residual distances \(32\) and \(36\), for which several comparison results are established. In the minimum-\(48\) case, the paper proves the exact inequality \(A_{64}+21A_{48}\ge 533\) together with an intersection bound for reduced contact quadrics.

The extremal problem \(u(8)=168\) is the main motivation for these results, but the manuscript does not claim the final upper bound \(u(8)\le 168\). The remaining residual configurations are stated explicitly in the paper.

The manuscript also contains exact finite computations used in several coding-theoretic arguments. Machine-readable certificates and verifier scripts are prepared as supplementary material; all submission-level computations use exact integer or rational arithmetic.

We believe the subject of the manuscript is within the scope of the journal, involving nodal surfaces, even sets of nodes, divisible codes, symmetric determinantal representations, and projective geometry.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
