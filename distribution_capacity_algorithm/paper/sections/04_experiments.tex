\section{IEEE 33-bus numerical study}\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Benchmark and evidence level}
The numerical study uses the 32 active branches of MATPOWER case33bw~\cite{matpower33}, with 12.66 kV and 10 MVA bases. The nominal active/reactive loads are multiplied by 0.65. Four PV inverters are placed at buses 9, 18, 24 and 33, each with 0.25 MW nominal active injection, a right-half-disc uncertainty radius of 0.125 MVA in the active/reactive plane and a 1 MVA nameplate. Extra unity-power-factor demand enters at bus 18. Voltage limits are 0.95--1.05 pu. We use a \emph{research-assumed} identical branch-current limit of 0.31 pu, which is \emph{not} a physical line-by-line ampacity dataset supplied by MATPOWER. Four gains are varied within an illustrative $[0,4]^4$ design box. These numerical assumptions must accompany every comparison.

The archival implementation computes the existing nonlinear implicit sensitivity and block-Hessian envelopes, the geometric current upper bound with an angular-grid allowance, and the correlated raw-PV capability bound on load cells. It adaptively refines every load cell from zero to each tested candidate. The IEEE 33-bus \emph{inner} numbers in Table~\ref{tab:results} use \emph{ordinary double precision}. They therefore demonstrate numerical behavior of conservative formulas but are \emph{not a formal outward-rounded all-uncertainty safety proof}.

On the same reported conservative algorithmic objective, the revised heterogeneous setting increases the accepted target by
\[
1.1500-1.0586=0.0914\ \mathrm{MW}=91.4\ \mathrm{kW},

This is \emph{not} evidence of an 8.63\% improvement in the rigorously proved \emph{true physical} maximum, because the new inner-side IEEE 33-bus calculations have not yet been validated with directed intervals. The last bus-33 gain touches the arbitrarily chosen design-box limit of 4; it cannot be advertised as a globally optimal hardware controller.

\subsection{Why structured current and voltage envelopes both matter}
The scalar second-derivative current estimate can be unnecessarily loose even when it is mathematically valid. In an earlier gain-$2.6$ cell near $1.00$--$1.02$ MW, the first-line scalar Hessian current estimate was roughly $0.334$ pu, exceeding the illustrative $0.31$ pu limit, whereas the affine phasor geometry with its nonlinear remainder yielded about $0.286$ pu for the same cell. The line-specific phasor enclosure thus removed a false rejection from the scalar bound. This comparison is \emph{for bounding methods}, not a claim that the actual physical current equals $0.286$ pu or that all other security conditions pass at that load.

At the same time the block Hessian is not discarded: it controls nonlinear bus voltage and the voltage-dependent inverter apparent-power envelope; a fully scalar phasor treatment cannot replace those derivative checks. The gain search is therefore \emph{capacity design using a structured oracle}, not merely tuning gains according to one sampled worst voltage.

\subsection{Separate physical unsafe-witness validation}
For the revised coordinated gains, consider the \emph{single feasible} disturbance $w_{p,j}=0$, $w_{q,j}=-0.0125$ pu for each PV and added load $1.1870$ MW. A separate interval fixed-point calculation on the intended physical normal branch encloses an equilibrium with all PV modes strictly unsaturated and
\[
0.94993228<V_{18}<0.94993334<0.95\ \mathrm{pu}.
\]
Thus for \emph{this fixed gain vector} there is a genuine violating witness at that load. It can obstruct a connected safe prefix beyond $1.1870$ MW; it does not prove that all disturbances below that load are safe, that other gains cannot perform better, or that all AC solutions are globally unique. The interval witness verifier remains in the accompanying numerical experiment archive; it has not yet been committed as a dependency-closed IEEE 33-bus solver to this paper folder.

A further gain-independent necessary outer bound $L^\star\le1.452831$ MW for the exact-decimal study feeder is recorded with a standalone exact-rational script in the existing research project. It is supplementary context, not an algorithmic optimality claim or evidence that our capacity-search procedure nearly attains the physical limit.

\subsection{Needed submission-stage ablation studies}
The most informative comparisons for an \emph{algorithm} paper will hold every physical assumption fixed and ablate only the envelope mechanism: (i) isotropic whole-Hessian voltage/current bounds; (ii) load/uncertainty block Hessians with scalar thermal estimates; (iii) block Hessians plus phasor current geometry; and (iv) the full algorithm with the joint PV disk test. Recording per-constraint limiting margins, computation time, load-cell subdivisions, angular resolution and conservative capacity should identify the actual contribution of each ingredient. A second feeder and realistic line-specific current limits are needed to make the engineering generality credible. We have not filled in absent running times, fabricated matched literature benchmark results or relabeled Monte Carlo stress tests as robust certificates.

\section{Discussion and conclusion}
The proposed method returns a concrete admission decision: a gain vector (which may be fixed), a load prefix, and full-cell feasibility records. This is a useful algorithmic output even without proving that it maximizes the true physical load-hosting capacity. Its theoretical contribution is the sound \emph{composition} of exact half-disc support, nonlinear implicit block-Hessian bounds, geometric line-current envelopes and a joint inverter capability consistency test. Its operational distinction is accepting the \emph{entire} loading trajectory in parameter space, not a finite grid of unrelated passing points.

Three gaps delimit the present claim. First, the IEEE 33-bus positive-capacity records are only floating-point candidates; rigorous outward-rounded state, Jacobian, Hessian, phasor and PV-envelope evaluation remains to be implemented. Second, when Q-clipping occurs within a cell, the present smooth oracle returns UNKNOWN rather than proving a valid piecewise mode; saturation-aware coverage is a logical extension, not a finished algorithmic result on this feeder. Third, a steady secure solution says nothing by itself about inverter transients, ramp limits, or dynamic stability.

The natural next paper milestone is therefore \emph{a reproducible validated IEEE 33-bus safe-inner run plus matched algorithmic ablations}, not a theorem about global gain optimality. The present manuscript supplies the full algorithmic question, mathematical construction, proof obligations and transparent numerical starting point.
